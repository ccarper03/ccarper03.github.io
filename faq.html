Buying here

Do you have homes ready now?

D.R. Horton builds first. So at any time, there can be homes ready now, homes being built, and homesites starting soon. What's ready changes week to week, so ask me what's open today.

How long does a home take?

A home that's ready now can close soon. A home that's being built depends on how far along it is. A homesite that's starting soon takes about seven months. That gives you time to sell your home or finish your lease.

What comes with the home?

From the current Collins Ridge features sheet:

  • Smart home: video doorbell, keypad front door lock and smart thermostat
  • Kitchen: quartz counters, tile backsplash, and a stainless gas range, microwave and dishwasher
  • 9-foot ceilings on the first floor (most plans)
  • James Hardie siding and a landscape package
  • A 10-year structural warranty

The fridge, washer, dryer and blinds aren't included. Features can change, so ask me for the latest, or see Collins Ridge on drhorton.com.

Is there a design center?

No. The floor plans are already designed, from building a lot of homes and listening to buyers. If your home is early in the build, you may get to pick one of three interior color schemes. Outside colors are set by homesite, so the street looks good together. Fewer choices means less stress and a faster move.

Can I buy if I still have a home to sell?

Yes, there are a few ways to do it. One is a home-sale contingency. At D.R. Horton, it comes with rules. The new home stays on the market while you sell. If another buyer makes an offer, you get a short window to drop the contingency, or the deal can end. Ask me how it works before you choose it. More on buying with a home to sell

Can I get my own home inspection?

Yes. You can hire your own licensed home inspector to check the home before the drywall goes up, and again before closing. A friend or family member who knows some construction doesn't count. We can't stop work for an inspection, so it has to fit our build schedule. Tell me early, and I'll help you line it up with our superintendent.

What happens with what my inspector finds?

You or your inspector can send the report straight to our superintendent. Reports often list a lot of items, and that's normal. We build every home to the local building code. If something doesn't meet code, we'll take care of it. Some items are suggestions or preferences rather than code issues. For those, our superintendent looks at each one and decides what we can do. Our superintendent will get back to you on the items.

How does the warranty work?

Every home comes with a 10-year structural warranty. After you move in, you send warranty requests through drhorton.com/warranty. More on what the warranty covers

HOA

This is a plain summary of the recorded Collins Ridge HOA documents, not the documents themselves. Check current rules with HOA management, or ask me and I'll show you where it's written.

What do the dues cover?

The shared spaces. That means the entrances, the community landscaping, any streets the town doesn't take care of, and the stormwater ponds. Dues also pay for the HOA's insurance, its management, and savings for future repairs. Ask me for the current amounts.

Why are townhome dues different?

On townhomes, the HOA takes care of the lawns, the shrubs and the outside of the buildings. On single-family homes, you take care of your own yard.

Can dues go up?

Yes. Once homeowners take over the HOA from the builder, yearly dues can't go up more than 20% over the year before.

What's a special assessment?

A one-time charge for a cost the yearly budget doesn't cover, like a big repair.

Can I put up a fence?

Yes, but get approval from the Architectural Review Committee (ARC) before you put it in. The Architectural Guidelines set the style, the height and where it can go.

Can I add a shed?

Permanent sheds aren't allowed.

Can I put in a pool?

An in-ground pool needs ARC approval. Above-ground pools aren't allowed.

Can I add solar panels?

Yes, with ARC approval.

Can I change my exterior color?

Repainting in the same approved colors doesn't need approval. A new color scheme needs ARC approval.

Who cuts the grass?

On single-family homes, you do, including the strip by the curb. On townhomes, the HOA does.

Can I park on the street?

Not on a regular basis. Park in your driveway and garage. RVs, boats and trailers have to be screened from view, and commercial trucks aren't allowed.

Can I have pets?

Yes, a reasonable number of household pets. Outside, dogs must be on a leash or fenced in, and pets should be licensed and vaccinated. The declaration doesn't permit Rottweilers, pit bulls or chows. Assistance animals for people with disabilities are handled under fair housing law, and breed or pet limits generally don't apply to them. Ask HOA management about a reasonable accommodation. If you have questions about your pet, reach out and I'll walk you through what the documents say.

Can I rent my home out?

Yes, with a written lease of at least six months. No short-term rentals.

Can I run a business from home?

Yes, as long as living there comes first. No signs, nothing that bothers the neighbors, and it has to follow local law.

Can I fly a flag or put up a political sign?

Yes, both are allowed.

Can I put up a basketball hoop or swing set?

Yes. The ARC reviews where it goes.

How do I get the full rules?

Ask the HOA. They'll send you the current Rules and Architectural Guidelines.

Inside the community

A saltwater pool. A clubhouse with a gym, a kitchen and a meeting room. Two tennis courts that double as pickleball courts. A dog park, walking trails and playgrounds.

Nearby outdoors

Occoneechee Mountain State Natural Area, the Hillsborough Riverwalk along the Eno, Gold Park and Eno River State Park.

Schools

Orange County Schools decides school assignments, and they can change. Check any address with the district's Assignment Locator on the Orange County Schools enrollment page.
