Buying here

Do you have homes ready now?

Homes here are built first. So at any time, there can be homes ready now, homes being built, and homesites starting soon. What's ready changes week to week, so ask me what's open today.

How long does a home take?

A home that's ready now can close soon. A home that's being built depends on how far along it is. A homesite that's starting soon takes several months to build. Ask me for the current estimate on the homesite you like. A longer build can give you time to sell your home or finish your lease.

What comes with the home?

Every home comes with a standard features list. It covers the kitchen, baths, floors, smart home tech, outside finishes and the warranty. The list changes over time, so ask me for the current one, and get it in writing before you sign. The model is staged, so some things you see there don't come with the home.

Is there a design center?

No. The floor plans are already designed, from building a lot of homes and listening to buyers. If your home is early in the build, you may get to pick from a few designer color schemes. Outside colors are set by homesite, so the street looks good together. Fewer choices means less stress and a faster move.

Can I buy if I still have a home to sell?

Yes. There are a few ways to do it, from selling first to a home-sale contingency to a bridge loan. The builder sets its own rules for contingencies, and they can change, so ask me how it works on the home you like before you choose. Get your moving plan

Can I get my own home inspection?

Yes. You can hire your own licensed home inspector to check the home before the drywall goes up, and again before closing. A friend or family member who knows some construction doesn't count. Work doesn't stop for an inspection, so it has to fit the build schedule. Tell me early, and I'll help you line it up with the builder's superintendent.

What happens with what my inspector finds?

You or your inspector can send the report straight to the builder's superintendent. Reports often list a lot of items, and that's normal. The superintendent reviews each item and gets back to you. What gets fixed, and how, is set by your purchase agreement and the builder's warranty, so read both.

How does the warranty work?

Homes come with a builder warranty, including long-term structural coverage. Your warranty book has the terms and the timing. After you move in, you send requests through the builder’s online warranty portal. I’ll show you where before you close. More on what the warranty covers

HOA

HOA rules live in the recorded declaration, the rules and the Architectural Guidelines. They can be amended, so I don't repeat them here. Here's what to know, and how to get the current version.

What do the dues cover?

The shared spaces: the entrances, community landscaping, amenities and stormwater ponds. Dues also pay for the HOA's insurance, its management, and savings for future repairs. For the current amount and budget, ask HOA management or ask me.

Why are townhome dues different?

On townhomes, the association takes care of more of the outside. The declaration spells out exactly what. On single-family homes, you take care of your own yard.

Can dues go up?

Yes. Budgets change with costs like insurance and upkeep. The declaration sets how increases work. Ask for the current budget before you buy.

What's a special assessment?

A one-time charge for a cost the yearly budget doesn't cover, like a big repair.

Can I add a fence, shed, pool or solar panels?

Most changes to the outside of your home need approval from the Architectural Review Committee first, and some may not be allowed. That includes fences, sheds, pools, solar panels, play sets and new paint colors. The Architectural Guidelines set the details. Get the current version from HOA management before you plan anything.

Are there rules on pets, parking and rentals?

Yes. The declaration and rules cover pets, parking, rentals and running a business from home. Read the current versions before you buy, especially if any of these matter to you. Assistance animals for people with disabilities are handled under fair housing law. Ask HOA management about a reasonable accommodation.

How do I get the full rules?

Ask HOA management for the current declaration, rules and Architectural Guidelines. They're the final word. If you'd like, I'll show you where a topic is covered before you buy.
