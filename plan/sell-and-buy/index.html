Estimates for planning, not lending or financial advice. Your lender decides what you qualify for, and your listing agent has your real selling costs. Each D.R. Horton home has its own rules for contingencies, and they can change.

How the math works
  • Equity is what your home might sell for, minus what you owe. Cash left after the sale takes out your selling costs and any extra cost that way carries.
  • Sell first and home-sale contingency assume you list right away. List later and the gap between homes shrinks, but so does your cushion if the sale runs long.
  • Buy first allows about two weeks to get your old home ready after you move, then your months to sell. Each month counts your full house payment, so it runs a little high.
  • The buy-before-you-sell program uses one national program's published terms from October 2026: a fee of 2.25% of your home's list price, about $1,850 in closing costs, no interest for the first 6 months, and total debt on your home of no more than 75% of its value. Programs change and differ, so get the real numbers in writing.
  • A home-sale contingency comes with rules from the builder. Often the new home stays on the market while you sell. If another buyer makes an offer, you get a short window to drop the contingency, or the contract can end.
  • Your lender decides what you qualify for. A lender counts your current house payment until your home is sold, or under contract with your buyer's loan approved. Source: Fannie Mae Selling Guide B3-6-05.
