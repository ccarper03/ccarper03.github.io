## Is it better to buy a new construction or existing home?

It depends on what you value and how long you'll stay. Neither one wins on price alone.

A resale home often wins on lot size, mature trees and an established street. It can also close faster. A new home wins on condition, layout and efficiency, and you pick more of it yourself.

The mistake I see most is comparing two list prices and stopping there. A lower price on a 15-year-old house can hide a five-figure to-do list. A higher price on a new house can hide extras you still have to buy, like blinds and a refrigerator. Line them up the same way and the answer gets clearer.

## The comparison that actually works

Put both homes in two columns and fill in the same rows for each.

| Row | What to look for |
| --- | --- |
| Price | The list or base price, plus lot premium and options on the new home |
| Cash to close | Down payment, closing costs, minus any seller or builder credits |
| Monthly payment | Principal, interest, taxes, insurance, HOA and mortgage insurance |
| Utilities | Ask for the last 12 months of bills on the resale. Ask for an energy rating on the new home |
| Big-ticket repairs | Age of the roof, HVAC, water heater and appliances on the resale |
| What's missing | Blinds, appliances, fencing, landscaping, a finished patio |
| Warranty | What's covered on each, and for how long |

Get the monthly numbers from a lender, not from me. Your rate, taxes and insurance are specific to you.

## Why the age of the systems matters so much

Every major part of a house has a lifespan. InterNACHI, the home inspectors' association, publishes typical ranges with normal maintenance:

- Architectural asphalt shingle roof: about 30 years. Three-tab shingles: about 20.
- Heat pump: 10 to 15 years. Central air conditioner: 7 to 15 years.
- Conventional tank water heater: 6 to 12 years.
- Dishwasher: about 9 years. Refrigerator: 9 to 13 years.
- Exterior paint: 7 to 10 years.

Run that against a home built 12 to 15 years ago. The water heater is at or past its range. The heat pump is close. The roof might have 5 to 15 years left. None of that shows up in the list price, but all of it shows up in your first five years.

On a new home, those clocks start at zero. That's the real advantage, and it's worth putting a number on.

## Is it cheaper to build or buy a house in 2026?

Usually, buying an existing home or a builder's home costs less than a custom build on your own land. It also takes less time. A custom build means paying for land, design, permits and a contractor yourself, and it takes longer.

The Census Bureau tracks this. For single-family homes finished in 2025, homes built for sale took about 7.4 months from permit to completion. Custom homes took 11.7 months with a hired contractor and 14.3 months when the owner acted as builder.

Cost-wise, it helps to know where a new home's price goes. The National Association of Home Builders' 2024 cost survey found construction was about 64% of the average sales price and the finished lot about 14%. The rest went to overhead, financing, marketing, commissions and builder profit. A production builder spreads design and land development across a whole community. A one-off custom build carries all of it alone.

"Build" can also mean picking a lot and plan from a builder in a new community. That's a different thing from a custom build, and it usually prices closer to buying.

## What devalues a house the most?

Condition does the most damage, and it's the part you control. Appraisers rate condition on a scale from C1 to C6. Fannie Mae's guidelines define C1 as new and never lived in, with no physical depreciation. Homes slide down the scale as maintenance gets put off.

The things that pull value down most often:

- **Deferred maintenance.** Roof leaks, a failing HVAC system, rot and water damage.
- **Systems at the end of their life.** Buyers price in the replacement.
- **Unpermitted work.** It can complicate a sale or require fixes before closing.
- **A layout that doesn't fit how people live now.** Closed-off kitchens and too few bathrooms are hard to fix.
- **Location factors you can't change.** Heavy traffic, noise, or a lot that drains poorly.

New homes aren't immune. A new home that isn't maintained loses its edge fast. But you start at the top of the scale instead of somewhere in the middle.

## How I'd use this

If you're torn between a resale listing and a new build, ask the listing agent for the age of the roof, the HVAC and the water heater, and the last year of utility bills. Ask the builder what's included and what isn't. Then put both homes through the table above with real numbers from your lender.
