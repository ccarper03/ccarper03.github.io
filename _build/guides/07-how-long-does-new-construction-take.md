## The real numbers

The Census Bureau's Survey of Construction tracks how long homes take. NAHB's summary of the 2025 data:

| Type of home | Permit to completion |
| --- | --- |
| All single-family homes | 8.8 months (1.4 months permit to start, 7.4 months start to finish) |
| Built for sale (like a builder's community) | 7.4 months |
| Custom, with a hired contractor | 11.7 months |
| Custom, owner as builder | 14.3 months |
| Built for rent | 12.4 months |

The South Atlantic region, which includes North Carolina, was the fastest of any region at 7.6 months.

## How long does it normally take to build a 2000 sq ft house?

For a home built for sale, plan on several months from permit to completion. The Census figure of 7.4 months is a good starting point. Size matters less than you'd think. What matters more is the stage the home is in when you buy it, the builder's schedule, and the weather.

The newest Census figures put the median new home at about 2,142 square feet, so a 2,000 square foot home is close to typical.

## To-be-built vs. move-in ready

You don't always have to wait the full build.

- **To-be-built:** You pick a homesite and plan, and the clock starts. You get the most choices.
- **Under construction:** The home is already started. Fewer choices, sooner closing.
- **Move-in ready:** Finished or nearly finished. The fastest path, with the finishes already picked.

Ask the builder for all three. A home that's halfway done might fit your timeline and your taste.

## Which stages take longest

In my experience on job sites, the waits are usually between stages, not inside them:

- **Before the start:** permits and site work.
- **Inspections:** each one has to pass before the next stage begins.
- **Interior finishes:** drywall, cabinets, flooring, paint and trim involve the most separate trades.
- **Weather:** rain stops grading, foundations, roofing and exterior work.

## Why is construction so slow in 2026?

It may be faster than you think. The Census data shows single-family construction times declined in 2025 compared with the year before.

When an individual home runs slow, the usual causes are local: weather, an inspection that needs a re-check, a back-ordered item, or a trade crew stretched across several jobs. Ask the builder what's driving a delay and what the new date is.

## Is new construction slowing down?

It depends on the market and the month. The Census Bureau publishes new residential construction numbers every month, and they move up and down.

For a buyer, what matters more is what's happening in the community you're looking at: how many homes are available, how many are under construction, and what's planned next.

## Planning around a build

- **Your lease:** Leave a cushion. Build dates move.
- **Your rate lock:** A standard lock may not cover a long build. Ask your lender about options.
- **Your current home:** A build window can give you time to sell.
