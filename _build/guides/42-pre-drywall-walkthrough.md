## Is a pre-drywall inspection worth it?

Yes. I've stood in a lot of framed houses, and this is the moment I'd least want a buyer to skip.

Once insulation and drywall go up, the framing, wiring, pipes and ducts disappear for good. InterNACHI makes the point plainly: fixing problems after a home is finished is difficult and expensive. Before drywall, many of the same fixes are quick.

There are two versions:

- **A builder walkthrough.** Many builders walk buyers through the home at this stage to review the layout and what's been installed.
- **A private inspection.** Your own inspector looks at the work on your behalf. Ask your builder if outside inspectors are allowed at this stage and how to schedule one.

## What happens during a pre-drywall inspection?

InterNACHI says the inspection happens after the foundation, framing, windows, doors and plumbing and electrical rough-ins are in, but before drywall. Some inspectors also look after insulation, which shows how well that was done, though insulation can hide other things.

Inspectors commonly check:

- **Framing:** wall studs, floor joists, notches and holes cut in framing, and blocking where extra support is needed
- **Structure:** footings, pier pads, crawl space, and missing metal clips or connectors
- **Water:** window and door flashing, and waterproofing
- **Plumbing:** pipe placement, and a drain pan under the washer location
- **Electrical:** wiring and junction box placement, and whether each room has enough outlets
- **HVAC:** duct and register placement
- **Fire safety:** firestop material where it belongs

InterNACHI also recommends photos and video. I'd take your own too.

## What to check yourself

Even if you hire an inspector, walk the house with your own list:

- **Outlets and switches** where you'll actually use them. Think about your bed, desk, TV and kitchen counters.
- **TV and cable locations**, and blocking in the wall if you'll mount a TV.
- **Blocking for grab bars, shelves or heavy mirrors.**
- **Ceiling fan locations** with the right support.
- **Your selections.** Make sure what's roughed in matches what you picked.

Take photos of every wall in every room. Years from now, when you want to hang something heavy or run a wire, you'll know exactly where the studs and pipes are.

## How long does a pre-drywall inspection take?

Usually a few hours, depending on the size of the home and the inspector. A builder walkthrough is often shorter. Plan for enough time to walk every room without rushing.

## Timing matters

The pre-drywall window is short. Drywall often follows insulation within days. Ask your builder:

- When do you expect framing and rough-ins to be finished?
- How much notice will I get to schedule an inspection?
- How do you handle items the inspection finds?
