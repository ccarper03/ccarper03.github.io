## Which direction should a house face?

The direction the front door faces matters less than where the sun hits the rooms and yard you use most.

Think about the back yard and your main living spaces:

- **Back yard faces north:** a shadier yard and patio. Cooler afternoons, and the front of the house gets the sun.
- **Back yard faces south:** a sunny yard for most of the day. Good for gardens. A patio may need shade in summer.
- **Back yard faces east:** morning sun, afternoon shade on the patio. Many people like this for summer evenings outside.
- **Back yard faces west:** a shady morning patio and strong late-afternoon sun. Beautiful sunsets, hot summer evenings.

## Which side of the house facing is not good?

No side is bad. Each has a trade-off. The one people most often regret in a hot climate is lots of unshaded glass facing west. Late-afternoon summer sun comes in low and is hard to block with a roof overhang, so those rooms can run warm.

## What the Department of Energy says about sun

The DOE's guidance on passive solar design explains how south-facing windows work: they collect the most winter sun, and a properly sized roof overhang can shade them in summer. It recommends solar-collecting windows face within 30 degrees of true south, and warns against oversizing south glass because modern homes have small heating loads. It also notes most climates need some form of summer shading.

You don't need a passive solar home to use the idea. Shaded south windows and fewer large west windows make a home easier to keep comfortable.

## Which direction is best for a house to face in Florida?

The principles are the same anywhere hot: limit unshaded west and east glass, shade the south side, and put your outdoor living where afternoon sun won't bake it. Florida's long cooling season makes those choices matter even more. In North Carolina, we have real winters too, so some winter sun through south windows is welcome.

## How to check a homesite

1. Stand on the lot and use your phone's compass to find which way the back faces.
2. Picture where the patio and main windows will be.
3. Visit in the late afternoon to see the sun and shade.
4. Ask what will be built to the south and west of you. Future homes and trees change the light.
