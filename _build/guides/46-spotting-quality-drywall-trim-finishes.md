## How to spot quality finishes

Finish work is what you see every day, so it's worth a close look. In good light, check:

- **Walls and ceilings.** Smooth and flat, with no seams, ridges or dents you can see. Hold your phone's flashlight flat against the wall and shine it along the surface. Bumps show up fast.
- **Corners.** Straight and clean, inside and out.
- **Trim.** Tight joints at corners, nail holes filled, and no gaps between the trim and the wall.
- **Caulk.** Smooth, unbroken lines at counters, tubs, trim and windows.
- **Paint.** Even coverage, clean lines where colors meet, no drips.
- **Doors.** Even gaps around the edges, and they latch without a push.

Most of these are normal punch list items, and builders expect to fix them. D.R. Horton builds first, so on a home that's ready now, you can check all of this before you buy.

## How many nail pops are normal in drywall?

There's no set number. A few in a new house is common. What matters is why they happen and how your warranty handles them.

A drywall guide from Canada Mortgage and Housing Corporation explains it well. The wood in the framing dries out after the house is closed in, and it shrinks as it dries. When a stud shrinks, it pulls away from the drywall, and the nail or screw head pushes out. The guide says this is usually worst in the first year, while the house dries out.

## Is it important to fix nail pops in drywall?

Not right away. They're cosmetic. Fixing them too early can mean fixing them twice, since more can show up while the house dries.

That's why many builders do drywall touch-ups once, near the end of the first year. At D.R. Horton, warranty requests go through drhorton.com/warranty. Around 10 months, you can schedule your warranty visit for things like nail pops. Keep a list as you go.

## Are nail pops in the ceiling normal?

Yes, in a new home they're common. The same guide explains something called truss uplift. In winter, the bottom of a roof truss stays warmer and drier than the top, so it can bow up a little. That mostly shows up as small cracks where the ceiling meets an inside wall. They often open in winter and close in summer.

## Do nail pops mean foundation problems?

Usually not. Nail pops here and there come from the framing drying and shrinking.

Signs worth a closer look:

- Cracks wider than a hair, or ones that keep growing
- Cracks running at an angle from the corners of doors and windows
- Doors and windows that suddenly stick or won't latch
- Floors with a slope you can notice
- Any crack with water or a stain

If you see any of those, report them to the warranty team in writing.

## Keep a running list

Walk the house once a month in the first year. Write down anything new, with a photo and the date. When it's time for your warranty visit, you'll have it all in one place.
