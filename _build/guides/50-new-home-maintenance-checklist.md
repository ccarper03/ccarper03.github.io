## What voids a builder's warranty?

Every warranty is different, so read yours. Common reasons a request gets turned down, or coverage is lost:

- **Neglect.** Damage from skipped upkeep, like a clogged AC drain line or full gutters.
- **Changing the drainage.** Building flower beds against the foundation, or blocking the low spots that carry water away.
- **Changes by someone else.** Work by your own contractor on a covered system can affect coverage for that item.
- **Missing the deadline.** Reporting after a coverage period ends.
- **Not following the steps.** The FTC suggests putting warranty requests in writing and keeping records.

The FTC also notes that builder warranties often leave out appliances, small cracks in drywall, tile or concrete, and items covered by a maker's warranty.

## Every month

- Check the air filter. ENERGY STAR suggests checking, cleaning or changing it about once a month.
- Run water in sinks and showers you don't use, so the drains don't dry out.
- Press the test button on the GFCI outlets.

## Spring

- Schedule a cooling tune-up. ENERGY STAR suggests booking it in spring, before contractors get busy.
- Clean the gutters, and make sure the downspouts carry water away from the house.
- Walk around the outside: siding, trim, and caulk around windows and doors.
- Check the ground. It should slope away from the foundation.
- Clear plants and leaves from around the outside AC unit.
- Test the smoke and carbon monoxide alarms.

## Summer

- Keep the AC drain line clear so it drains the way it should.
- Watch for standing water in the yard after storms.
- If you have sprinklers, make sure they aren't spraying the house.

## Fall

- Schedule a heating tune-up. ENERGY STAR suggests fall, before winter.
- Clean the gutters again after the leaves drop.
- Unhook hoses and cover outside faucets before it freezes.
- Check caulk and weatherstripping before it gets cold.
- Test the smoke and carbon monoxide alarms.

## Winter

- Watch for water on the inside of windows. It's a sign the air inside is too humid.
- Keep an eye on drywall cracks. Small ones that open in dry winter air are normal in a new home.
- Know where your main water shutoff is, in case a pipe freezes.

## Keep a home log

A notebook, or a note on your phone: the date, what you did, who did it, and receipts. It helps with warranty requests, and it's nice to have when you sell.
