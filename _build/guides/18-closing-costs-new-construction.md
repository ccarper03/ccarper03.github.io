## What closing costs cover

Closing costs are everything it takes to finish the loan and put the home in your name, on top of the price and your down payment. They usually include:

- **Lender fees.** Origination fees and, if you choose them, discount points.
- **Outside services.** The appraisal, credit report, a survey if needed, and a flood check.
- **Title.** The title search and title insurance.
- **The closing attorney.** In North Carolina, attorneys handle closings.
- **Government fees.** Recording fees, and the state excise tax, which the seller pays.
- **Prepaid items.** Your first year of homeowners insurance, and a first deposit into escrow for taxes and insurance.

Freddie Mac estimates closing costs at about 2% to 5% of the price.

## Who normally pays closing costs in NC?

Each side pays its own, unless the contract says otherwise.

**The seller usually pays:**

- **The excise tax.** North Carolina law sets it at $1 for every $500 of the price, paid by the seller.
- **Preparing the deed**, and the seller's agent commission if there is one.

**The buyer usually pays:**

- Lender fees, the appraisal and credit report.
- Title insurance and the buyer's closing attorney.
- Recording fees for the deed of trust.
- Prepaid insurance, taxes and interest.

NC REALTORS notes that sellers are sometimes asked to pay some or all of the buyer's closing costs, depending on the offer. On a new home, that's where builder credits come in.

## Who pays closing costs on a new construction home?

The same split applies. The buyer pays the buyer's costs, and the builder, as the seller, pays the seller's costs. The difference is that builders often offer a credit toward the buyer's closing costs.

A few things to know about those credits:

- **They often come with conditions.** Many are tied to using the builder's lender, title company or closing attorney.
- **They have limits.** Loan programs cap how much a seller can pay. Fannie Mae, for example, caps it at 3%, 6% or 9% of the price or appraised value on a home you'll live in, depending on how much you borrow. A credit also can't be more than your actual closing costs.
- **They should be in writing.** The amount and the conditions belong in your contract or an addendum.

## How much are closing costs on a $400,000 home?

Using Freddie Mac's 2% to 5% estimate, about $8,000 to $20,000. That's a range for planning, not a quote.

Your real number depends on your loan type, whether you pay points, your insurance cost and how much escrow the lender collects up front. Builder credits can bring your share down.

The Loan Estimate you get after applying shows estimated closing costs and cash to close. The Closing Disclosure shows the final numbers. Federal rules say the lender has to give it to you at least three business days before closing.

## Is 10% closing cost normal?

No. Ten percent is well above Freddie Mac's usual 2% to 5% range. If your estimate looks that high, find out why.

Common reasons an estimate runs high:

- Discount points to buy down the rate
- A large escrow deposit, especially with a big insurance bill
- Prepaid interest from closing early in the month
- Fees stacked up from several companies

Ask the lender to walk you through each part. Then get a Loan Estimate from at least one other lender and compare them line by line.

## Check the numbers before closing day

1. Compare your Closing Disclosure to your Loan Estimate. The CFPB recommends it.
2. Make sure the builder credit shows up in the amount you were promised.
3. Check your cash to close and how to send it. Confirm wiring instructions by phone with your attorney's office, using a number you already know. Never trust instructions sent by email alone.
