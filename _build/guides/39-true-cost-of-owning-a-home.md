## What are the hidden costs of owning a house?

These never show up in the price:

- **Property taxes.** In North Carolina, you pay your county's rate, plus a town rate if you're inside town limits. Orange County lists both per $100 of assessed value. Inside Hillsborough, you pay both.
- **Homeowners insurance.** Usually collected through escrow with your mortgage.
- **Flood or wind coverage.** The NC Department of Insurance notes that wind, hail and flood may not be covered by a regular homeowners policy. You may need a separate one.
- **HOA dues**, if the community has an association.
- **Utilities.** Power, water and sewer, trash and internet.
- **Upkeep and repairs.** Filters, gutters, caulk, and someday the big replacements.
- **Escrow changes.** The CFPB notes that taxes and insurance can change each year, so your escrow payment can too.

## Planning for the big replacements

Every big part of a house wears out on its own schedule. InterNACHI lists typical lifespans, with normal upkeep:

| Item | Typical life |
| --- | --- |
| Architectural shingle roof | About 30 years |
| Heat pump | 10 to 15 years |
| Tank water heater | 6 to 12 years |
| Refrigerator | 9 to 13 years |
| Dishwasher | About 9 years |
| Outside paint | 7 to 10 years |
| Carpet | 8 to 10 years |

This is where new and older homes really differ. In a new home, those clocks start at zero, and the builder warranty covers certain problems early on. In a 12- to 15-year-old home, several of these could come due in your first few years.

A simple habit: set aside money every month for repairs. Set aside more for an older home than a new one.

## What salary do you need to live comfortably in North Carolina?

It depends on where you live in the state and who's in your household. The Triangle costs more than many smaller towns.

The MIT Living Wage Calculator estimates the wage a full-time worker needs to cover basic needs, by county and household type. It's a good floor. "Comfortable" means more than the basics: savings, a cushion, and room for things you enjoy.

For housing, the better question is what monthly cost fits your income. Lenders use your debt-to-income ratio to set a ceiling. Your own comfort number is usually lower.

## Is Raleigh, NC expensive to live?

It depends on what you compare it to, and housing is the piece that moves the most. The MIT calculator lets you compare counties side by side, so you can line up Wake, Durham and Orange for the same household.

Housing costs vary a lot by town and neighborhood. That's why many people look a little farther out. The trade-off is the drive. Before you decide, drive the commute at rush hour and price the full monthly cost, not just the house.

## Add up your full monthly number

Before you commit, add these up:

1. Principal and interest, from your lender
2. Property taxes, at the finished home's value
3. Homeowners insurance, plus any flood or wind policy
4. Mortgage insurance, if your down payment is small
5. HOA dues
6. Utilities, from real bills or estimates
7. A monthly amount set aside for upkeep and repairs

If that total still leaves room to save, you've found a home you can enjoy, not just afford.
