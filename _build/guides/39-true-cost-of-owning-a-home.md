## What are the hidden costs of owning a house?

The ones that never show up in a listing price:

- **Property taxes.** In North Carolina, you pay your county's rate, plus a town rate if you're inside town limits. Orange County publishes both per $100 of assessed value. Inside Hillsborough, the county and town rates add together.
- **Homeowners insurance.** Usually collected through escrow with your mortgage.
- **Separate flood or wind coverage.** The NC Department of Insurance notes that windstorm, hail and flood may be excluded from a primary homeowners policy, and a separate policy may be needed.
- **HOA dues**, if the community has an association.
- **Utilities.** Power, water and sewer, trash, internet.
- **Maintenance and repairs.** Filters, gutters, caulk, and eventually the big replacements.
- **Escrow changes.** Your payment can change when taxes or insurance change. The CFPB notes that since taxes and premiums can change each year, your escrow payment can too.

## Planning for the big replacements

Every major part of a house wears out on its own schedule. InterNACHI publishes typical lifespans, with normal maintenance:

| Item | Typical life |
| --- | --- |
| Architectural shingle roof | About 30 years |
| Heat pump | 10 to 15 years |
| Conventional tank water heater | 6 to 12 years |
| Refrigerator | 9 to 13 years |
| Dishwasher | About 9 years |
| Exterior paint | 7 to 10 years |
| Carpet | 8 to 10 years |

This is where new and older homes really differ. In a new home, those clocks start at zero, and a builder warranty covers certain defects early on. In a 12- to 15-year-old home, several of these could come due in your first few years.

A simple habit: set aside money every month for repairs, and more for an older home than a new one.

## What salary do you need to live comfortably in North Carolina?

It depends on where you live in the state and your household. Costs in the Triangle run differently from smaller towns.

The MIT Living Wage Calculator estimates the wage a full-time worker needs to cover basic needs by county, for different family types. It's a useful floor. "Comfortable" means more than basic needs: savings, a cushion, and room for things you enjoy.

For housing, the better question is what monthly cost fits your income. Lenders use your debt-to-income ratio to set a ceiling. Your own comfort number is usually lower.

## Is Raleigh, NC expensive to live?

It depends on what you compare it to, and housing is the piece that moves most. The MIT calculator lets you compare counties side by side, so you can line up Wake, Durham and Orange with the same household type.

Housing costs vary a lot by town and neighborhood, which is why many people look a little farther out. Commute time is the trade-off. Before you decide, drive the commute at rush hour and price the full monthly cost, not just the house.

## Build your full monthly number

Add these up before you commit:

1. Principal and interest, from your lender
2. Property taxes, at the full assessed value
3. Homeowners insurance, plus any flood or wind policy
4. Mortgage insurance, if your down payment is under the threshold
5. HOA dues
6. Utilities, using real bills or estimates
7. A monthly set-aside for maintenance and repairs

If that total still leaves room to save, you've found a home you can enjoy, not just afford.
