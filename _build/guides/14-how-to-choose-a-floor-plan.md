## Start with an ordinary Tuesday

Model homes are staged for the weekend: guests, holidays, a dinner party. You live in the house on Tuesday.

Walk through a normal weekday in your head, room by room:

- Where do groceries land, and how far is the pantry?
- Where does laundry happen, and how far is it from the bedrooms?
- Who works from home, and where does the door close?
- Where do shoes, bags and the dog go when you walk in?
- Who sleeps where, and who uses the stairs?

A plan that handles your Tuesday well will handle the holiday fine.

## The choices that are hard to change later

These are worth the most thought, because you can't swap them out in a weekend:

- **Number of bedrooms.** Count for the next five years, not just now.
- **Stairs.** A main-floor bedroom or primary suite matters to many downsizers and families with older parents.
- **A full bath on the main level.** NAHB's 2024 survey found 80% of buyers rated it essential or desirable.
- **Laundry location.** The most-wanted feature in that survey, rated essential or desirable by 86% of buyers.
- **Flex space.** A room that can be an office now and a bedroom later.
- **Storage.** A walk-in pantry, garage storage and closets. NAHB found 80% to 81% of buyers want a walk-in pantry and garage storage.

## What are the most popular floor plans?

Popular right now means open living areas with a separate place to get away. The features buyers rated highest in NAHB's 2024 *What Home Buyers Really Want* survey include a laundry room, a patio, a front porch, table space for eating, a walk-in pantry and a full bath on the main level.

The most popular plan in any community is usually the one that solves the most common household's needs at the price point. That doesn't make it the right one for you.

## What style of home has the highest resale value?

The one that fits what most buyers in that area want. There isn't a single winning style nationally.

What tends to help resale, from what buyers say they want:

- Enough bedrooms for the area's typical buyer
- A main-floor bathroom, and often a main-floor bedroom
- A usable laundry room and storage
- Outdoor space like a patio or porch
- A layout without wasted space

What tends to hurt is an unusual layout that only fits one household, or a home that's far bigger or smaller than its neighbors.

## What do realtors use for floor plans?

Most agents use the builder's published plan sheets for new construction. For resale homes, many use floor plan apps or measurement services that create a drawing from a scan or photos.

For your own decision, print the plan sheets and draw on them. Mark where your furniture goes, where you'll work and where the TV goes. It shows problems a walkthrough can miss.

## Use the plan, then walk the plan

1. Narrow it to two or three plans on paper.
2. Walk the model, and any finished homes in those plans.
3. Measure rooms against your furniture.
4. Then pick, and choose your options while they're still easy to add.
