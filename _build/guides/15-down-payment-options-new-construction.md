## Do you have to put 20% down on a new build?

No. Twenty percent is a milestone, not a rule.

On a conventional loan, the CFPB explains that private mortgage insurance is usually required when you put down less than 20 percent of the price. That's where the number comes from. It's the point where mortgage insurance usually goes away, not the least you need to buy.

New homes use the same loan programs as any other home. What changes is timing. A home that's starting soon takes months, so your down payment and your rate lock need to fit that schedule. A home that's ready now closes sooner.

## The main options, side by side

Each option has its own rules, and lenders add their own. Use this as a map, then talk to a lender about the details.

| Loan type | Down payment | Mortgage insurance | Good to know |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| VA loan | Usually none, if the price isn't above the appraised value | No PMI or MIP | For veterans, service members and some surviving spouses who qualify. A one-time funding fee may apply |
| FHA loan | HUD says it can be as low as 3.5% of the price | Yes, FHA mortgage insurance | Easier credit rules, per HUD. Popular with first-time buyers |
| Conventional loan | Depends on the program and lender | PMI when you put down less than 20% | Many lenders offer low down payment options |
| State help | Helps with the down payment | Depends on the main loan | NC Housing Finance Agency programs, through approved lenders |

## The VA loan, from one veteran to another

I served in the Marines, so this one's close to home. The VA says its purchase loan usually needs no down payment, as long as the price isn't higher than the appraised value. It also has no PMI or mortgage insurance premiums. There may be a one-time funding fee.

You'll need a Certificate of Eligibility. You'll have to meet VA and lender rules for credit and income, and you'll need to live in the home. The VA says the loan can be used to build, buy or improve a home, so a new home works.

## How much of a down payment do I need for a $400,000 house as a first-time buyer?

It depends on the loan type and the lender, not just the price. VA loans can need nothing down for buyers who qualify. HUD says FHA allows a down payment as low as 3.5% of the price. Conventional programs vary.

In North Carolina, the NC Housing Finance Agency offers down payment help through approved lenders. Its site lists the NC Home Advantage Mortgage, with help of up to 3% of the loan amount for first-time and move-up buyers. It also lists NC 1st Home Advantage, with a set dollar amount for first-time buyers. NCHFA counts you as a first-time buyer if you haven't owned a home you lived in during the past three years. Amounts and rules change, so check NCHFA's current terms.

## Is $10,000 a good amount to put down on a house?

It can be, depending on the price and the loan. What matters more is what you have left after closing.

A smaller down payment means a bigger loan and, usually, mortgage insurance. A bigger down payment lowers the loan but can leave you short on cash. You'll also need closing costs, earnest money, and money for things a new home may not include, like blinds and a fridge.

My advice: don't empty your savings to hit a down payment number. Keep a cushion. Then ask a lender to show you the payment at two or three down payment amounts, side by side.

## Questions to ask a lender

- Which loans do I qualify for, and how much does each need down?
- What will mortgage insurance cost, and how can I remove it later?
- Can a builder credit cover any of my closing costs?
- Can I get NCHFA help through your company?
- How does the down payment fit the timing of the home I like?
