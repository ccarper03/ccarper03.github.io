## Why this walkthrough matters

NC REALTORS reminds buyers that under North Carolina's standard purchase contract, closing means you accept the home as it is that day. A builder's contract has its own terms, but the idea is the same: find things before you close.

On a new home, the final walkthrough is often called a new home orientation. The builder shows you how the home works, and you make a list of touch-ups together. That list is called a punch list.

## What should I include on my final walkthrough checklist?

Bring painter's tape to mark spots, a phone charger to test outlets, and this list.

**Every room**

- Doors open, close and latch. Locks work.
- Windows open, close and lock. Screens are in. No cracked or foggy glass.
- Walls and ceilings: dents, scuffs, nail pops, seams you can see, missed paint.
- Floors: scratches, gaps, chips, loose tiles or squeaks.
- Trim and baseboards: gaps and missing caulk.
- Every outlet works, including the ones with test buttons (GFCI). Plug in your charger to check.
- Every light and switch works.
- Air comes out of every vent.

**Kitchen**

- Cabinets and drawers open, close and line up. No chips or scratches.
- Countertops: chips, scratches, seams.
- Every appliance that comes with the home turns on: range, oven, microwave, dishwasher, disposal.
- The faucet runs hot and cold, the sprayer works, and nothing leaks under the sink.

**Bathrooms**

- Faucets, showers and tubs: hot and cold water, good drains, no leaks.
- Toilets flush and refill. They don't rock.
- Fans work.
- Grout and caulk are finished.

**Systems**

- The thermostat runs the heat and the air.
- The water heater is on and labeled.
- The breaker panel is labeled.
- Smoke and carbon monoxide alarms are in.

**Outside**

- Siding, trim and paint.
- Gutters and downspouts carry water away from the house.
- The ground slopes away from the house.
- Driveway, walks and patio: cracks or chips.
- The garage door opens, closes, and backs up if something is in the way.

## How soon is the final walkthrough before closing?

It depends. On a resale home, it's often a day or two before closing. On a new home, builders often do it several days to a week or more ahead, so there's time to fix the punch list. Ask for the date as early as you can.

## Can a seller deny a final walk-through?

On a resale home in North Carolina, the standard contract lets the buyer get into the home, including for a final walk-through, up to closing or move-in. On a new home, the builder's contract decides. Builders almost always include a walkthrough or orientation, but read the contract so you know the terms.

## Before closing or under warranty

Ask the builder to split the punch list into two groups, in writing:

1. **Fixed before closing.** Usually touch-ups, and anything that affects how something works or safety.
2. **Fixed under warranty.** Things that need parts ordered, or that make more sense to do later.

Then check the first group again before closing day. If something you agreed on won't be done until after closing, NC REALTORS says to get a written agreement that says when and how.
