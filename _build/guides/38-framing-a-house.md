## Why framing is the stage I love

I've cut the lumber, framed the walls and stood on the trusses. Framing is when a slab turns into a house. Rooms show up in a day or two. You can stand in your future kitchen and look out the window openings.

It's also when a lot gets decided that you'll never see again.

## What do framing inspectors look for?

In North Carolina, framing is one of the routine inspections the local building department does, after the rough-ins and before insulation. The inspector checks the structure against the code. Common items:

- **Headers** over windows and doors, sized for the opening
- **Connectors**, like metal straps and clips that tie the roof to the walls
- **Wall bracing and sheathing**, which keep the house steady in wind
- **Fire-blocking** that slows fire from moving through walls and floors
- **Notches and holes** cut for pipes, wires and ducts, which can weaken framing if they're too big or in the wrong spot
- **Stairs**, including step height, depth and headroom

Your own inspector at this stage, before drywall, adds a second set of eyes on how well the work was done. InterNACHI's pre-drywall guidance lists things like the condition of studs and joists, notches, fire-blocking and missing metal clips. ## How much would it cost to frame a 2000 sq ft house?

It depends on the plan, the roof, the price of lumber and local labor. A simple box frames faster and costs less than a home with lots of corners, tall ceilings or a complex roof.

For scale, NAHB's 2024 survey found framing was about 16.6% of the cost to build the average new home. That's a better guide than a price per square foot, which moves with the lumber market.

## What to look at yourself

If you or your inspector see the home before drywall, look for:

- **The layout.** Doors, windows and walls where the floor plan says they should be.
- **Blocking.** Extra wood in the wall where you'll mount a TV, grab bars or heavy shelves.
- **Water.** Some rain on framing is normal. Standing water on floors, or soaked wood that never dries, isn't.
- **Damaged lumber.** Cracked studs or broken trusses should be replaced, not ignored.

Take photos of every wall before insulation and drywall go up. You'll use them for years.

## What should I check on my new build walkthrough checklist?

At the framing stage, use the list above. At the end, use a full room-by-room punch list for doors, windows, outlets, plumbing, finishes and the outside. My final walkthrough guide has one you can print.

## A word on nail pops and cracks later

Framing lumber dries and shrinks a little after the house is closed in. A Canadian housing agency's drywall guide explains that as studs dry, they can pull away from the drywall and cause nail pops, and that uneven shrinking can cause cracks, usually worst in the first year. That's normal settling. Your warranty visit near the end of the first year is the time to have it looked at.
