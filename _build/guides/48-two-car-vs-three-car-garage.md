## What percentage of Americans have a 3 car garage?

Among new homes, about 15%. NAHB's analysis of Census data on new single-family homes completed in 2024 found:

| Parking | Share of new homes |
| --- | --- |
| Two-car garage | 65% |
| Three or more car garage | 15% |
| One-car garage | 9% |
| No parking facility | 9% |
| Carport | 1% |

## Does a 3 car garage add value to a house?

It can, if buyers in your area want one. A third bay is useful for a third vehicle, a boat, a workshop, a gym or storage. In markets where most homes have two-car garages, a third bay can set a home apart.

It also costs more to build and needs a wider lot. If you won't use it, the money may do more elsewhere in the home.

## How much does not having a garage devalue your house?

There's no reliable single figure. It depends on the market and what buyers expect. In areas where nearly every comparable home has a garage, a home without one stands out, and not in a good way. Since about 89% of new homes in the 2024 data had a garage, it's a feature most buyers assume.

## The storage that actually matters

What people regret skipping isn't usually the third bay. It's storage:

- **Garage storage.** NAHB's 2024 survey found 81% of buyers rate it essential or desirable.
- **Garage depth.** A few extra feet makes room for a full-size truck plus a workbench or shelving. Measure your vehicles.
- **Attic access and decking.** Ask whether attic space is usable for storage.
- **A walk-in pantry and closets.** Also high on buyers' lists.
- **Outlets in the garage.** For a freezer, tools or a future EV charger. Easier to add during the build.

## What to check

- Will my vehicles fit with the doors open and room to walk around?
- Is there space for shelving, bikes and trash bins?
- Are there enough outlets, and is there a circuit for a charger later?
- Is a garage extension or a third bay offered on this plan and lot?
