## How to find new construction neighborhoods

The most common ways:

- **Builder websites.** Builders list their communities, plans and homes ready now.
- **Driving around.** New communities post signs and open model homes.
- **Home search websites.** Many show new homes next to resale homes.
- **An agent.** A buyer's agent can compare communities for you.
- **Town and county planning departments.** They show what's been approved and what's being reviewed.

Start with where you need to be. Pick the towns that fit your drive, then look at what's being built there.

## What are the qualities of a good neighborhood?

Judge a neighborhood by its places, homes and drive times, never by who lives there. The things that matter:

- **The daily drive.** To work, groceries, doctors and the places you go every week.
- **The streets and layout.** Sidewalks, traffic, street lights, and how the community connects to main roads.
- **Amenities.** A pool, trails, parks or a clubhouse, and whether they're done.
- **The HOA.** What it covers, what it costs and what rules come with it.
- **Upkeep.** How the common areas and finished sections are kept up.
- **What's next door.** Future phases, stores, roads or open space.

For schools, check the school district's lookup for the exact address, since assignments can change.

## Five things to compare

| Factor | What to ask |
| --- | --- |
| Drive | How long is it at rush hour? |
| HOA | What are the dues, what do they cover, and what are the rules? |
| Amenities | What's built, what's planned, and when? |
| Build-out | How many homes are done, being built and still to come? |
| Surroundings | What's planned on the land around the community? |

## The HOA

Most new communities have one. NAHB's look at Census data shows 65.7% of new single-family homes started in 2024 were in a community or homeowners association.

Read the declaration, rules and budget before you sign. Ask when control passes from the developer to the owners. North Carolina's Planned Community Act says owners must elect a board, with a majority of owners on it, no later than the end of the developer's control period.

## Early or late in the build-out

Buying early and buying late each have trade-offs. Early buyers often get first pick of homesites, and live with construction nearby. Late buyers get a finished neighborhood and fewer homesites to choose from. My guide on buying early covers it in more detail.

## Visit twice

See each community on a weekday evening and on a Saturday. Drive in from the direction you'll commute. Walk the finished streets, not just the model.
