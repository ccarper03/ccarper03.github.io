## How to find new construction neighborhoods

The most common ways:

- **Builder websites.** Builders list their active communities, plans and homes available now.
- **Driving the area.** New communities post signs and open model homes.
- **Listing portals.** Many include new construction alongside resale homes.
- **An agent.** A buyer's agent can compare builders and communities for you.
- **Town and county planning departments.** They show what's been approved and what's under review.

Start with where you need to be. Pick the towns that fit your commute, then look at what's being built there.

## What are the qualities of a good neighborhood?

Judge a neighborhood by places, homes and drive times, never by who lives there. The qualities that matter:

- **The daily drive.** To work, groceries, healthcare and the places you go every week.
- **The streets and layout.** Sidewalks, traffic patterns, street lighting, and how the community connects to main roads.
- **Amenities.** Pool, trails, parks, a clubhouse, and whether they're finished.
- **The HOA.** What it covers, what it costs and what rules come with it.
- **Upkeep.** How the common areas and finished sections are maintained.
- **What's next door.** Future phases, commercial land, roads or open space.

For schools, check the district's own assignment lookup for the specific address, since assignments can change.

## Five things to compare across communities

| Factor | What to ask |
| --- | --- |
| Commute | How long is the drive at rush hour? |
| HOA | What are the dues, what do they cover, and what are the rules? |
| Amenities | What's built, what's planned, and when? |
| Build-out | How many homes are finished, under construction and still to come? |
| Surroundings | What's planned on the land around the community? |

## The HOA, specifically

Most new communities have one. NAHB's analysis of Census data shows 65.7% of new single-family homes started in 2024 were in a community or homeowners association.

Read the declaration, rules and budget before you sign. Ask when control passes from the developer to the owners. North Carolina's Planned Community Act requires owners to elect a board, with a majority of owners, no later than the end of the developer's control period.

## Build-out stage

Buying early and buying late each have trade-offs. Early buyers often get first pick of homesites and live with construction nearby. Late buyers get a finished neighborhood and fewer choices. My guide on buying early covers this in detail.

## Visit twice

See each community on a weekday evening and a Saturday. Drive in from the direction you'll commute. Walk the finished streets, not just the model.
