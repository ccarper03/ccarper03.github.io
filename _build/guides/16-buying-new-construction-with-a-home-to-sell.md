## Is it possible to buy a new construction house before selling my current home?

Yes. Lots of people do. The real questions are how you'll qualify for the loan, and how you'll pay for two homes if they overlap.

Your lender has to count your current house payment when you apply for the new loan. If your home isn't under contract yet, you need to qualify with both payments. If you use a bridge loan, Fannie Mae counts it in your debt-to-income ratio, unless you show a signed contract on your current home with its financing contingencies cleared.

That's why the order matters.

## The four ways to do it

| Way | How it works | Best fit | Watch out for |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Sell first** | Sell, then buy with the cash in hand | You want one payment and no guessing | You may need a short-term place to live |
| **Buy first** | Buy, then sell | You qualify for both payments | Paying for two homes if the sale is slow |
| **Home-sale contingency** | Your purchase depends on selling your home by a deadline | You need the sale to buy | The new home can stay on the market while you sell |
| **Bridge or buy-before-you-sell program** | A lender or company helps you buy before your home sells | You have equity and want one move | Fees, rules and qualifying requirements |

At D.R. Horton, a home-sale contingency comes with rules. The new home stays on the market while you sell. If another buyer makes an offer, you get a short window to drop the contingency or the deal can end. Ask me how it works before you choose it.

## Can I sell my house and buy a new one at the same time?

You can try to close both on the same day or the same week. It takes teamwork between both closings, both attorneys and both lenders.

New construction helps here. D.R. Horton builds first, so you can pick a home that fits your timing:

- **Ready now** if you've already sold, or you're about to.
- **Being built** if your sale is a few months out.
- **Starting soon** if you need time. Plan on about seven months. For context, the Census Bureau found homes built for sale took about 7.4 months from permit to completion in 2025.

That window gives you time to get ready, list and sell while the new home goes up. Then you aim both closings at the same date.

The catch: build dates move. Weather, inspections and materials can push them. Leave yourself some room.

## Should I sell my current home before buying a new one?

Sell first if you can't comfortably pay for two homes, or if your down payment comes from the sale. Buy first if you can qualify for both and don't want to move twice.

For many of the downsizers and move-up buyers I talk with, the answer is in between. Pick a home that's starting soon or early in the build. List your current home partway through. Then close both close together. A good listing agent and a lender who has done this before make all the difference.

## Can you avoid capital gains by buying a new house?

Not by buying another home. What protects most homeowners is the home sale exclusion.

The IRS lets you leave out up to $250,000 of gain when you sell your main home, or up to $500,000 on a joint return. You generally need to have owned the home, and lived in it as your main home, for at least two of the five years before the sale. You generally can't use it if you used it on another home in the past two years.

If you don't meet the full test, a partial exclusion may apply when the main reason you sold was a new job, health or certain other events you couldn't plan for. Talk to a tax professional about your situation.

## Questions to ask before you choose

- Can I qualify with both payments if my home doesn't sell in time?
- How does a home-sale contingency work on the home I like?
- How much equity will I have after selling costs?
- What does a bridge or buy-before-you-sell program cost, and how does it count when I qualify?
- What's my backup plan if the build date or my sale date moves?
