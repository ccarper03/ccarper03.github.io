## What are the 7 stages of construction?

I spent almost two years on job sites, from grading to final finishes. Here's the order a house actually goes up in. The National Association of Home Builders tracks construction costs in these same stages, so I've included roughly how much of the construction budget each one takes, from NAHB's 2024 survey.

| Stage | What happens | Share of construction cost |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1. Site work | Clearing, grading, utilities, erosion control | About 8% |
| 2. Foundation | Footings, slab or crawl space, waterproofing | About 11% |
| 3. Framing | Walls, floors, roof trusses and sheathing | About 17% |
| 4. Rough-ins | Plumbing, electrical and HVAC run through the walls | About 19% |
| 5. Exterior finishes | Roofing, siding, windows, doors | About 13% |
| 6. Interior finishes | Insulation, drywall, trim, cabinets, flooring, paint | About 24% |
| 7. Final steps | Fixtures, landscaping, driveway, cleaning, final inspection | About 7% |

Insulation and drywall get grouped with interior finishes in NAHB's survey, but they matter so much I treat them as their own moment. Once drywall goes up, you can't see inside the walls again.

## The inspections along the way

North Carolina's building code lists the routine inspections a local building department performs:

1. Footing
2. Under slab, where it applies
3. Foundation
4. Rough-in
5. Building framing
6. Insulation
7. Final

Fire protection inspections apply to non-residential work. A recent state interpretation also notes that local departments can't add other routine inspections without approval.

Those inspections check code compliance. Many buyers also hire their own inspector at key stages, especially before drywall and before closing. Ask your builder what's allowed and when.

## How many man hours does it take to build a 1500 sq ft house?

There's no reliable standard number, and I'd be wary of anyone who gives you one. A house is built by many separate trade crews, each working days at a time, with gaps between them for inspections, deliveries and weather.

Time on the calendar is the more useful measure. The Census Bureau's data, reported by NAHB, shows single-family homes built for sale took about 7.4 months from permit to completion in 2025. Homes in the South Atlantic region, which includes North Carolina, were among the fastest.

## How long are new homes built to last?

The structure is built to last for generations with normal care. The parts inside it wear out on their own schedules.

InterNACHI's chart puts an architectural shingle roof at about 30 years, a heat pump at 10 to 15 years, and a tank water heater at 6 to 12 years. Those are guidelines that assume regular maintenance. Keep up with filters, gutters, caulk and drainage, and a new home gives you decades before the big replacements start.

## What I'd watch for as a buyer

- **Before drywall:** This is your one look inside the walls. Outlet locations, blocking for TV mounts, and plumbing and duct runs are all visible.
- **During exterior finishes:** Flashing around windows and doors keeps water out. It's worth a look.
- **At the end:** A careful final walkthrough and punch list.
