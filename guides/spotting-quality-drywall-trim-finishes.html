Drywall, Trim and Finishes: How to Spot Quality

By Charles CarperUpdated October 20263 min read

A few nail pops and small cracks are normal in a new home as the framing lumber dries and shrinks, usually in the first year. They're cosmetic. Look for straight, smooth walls, tight trim joints and complete caulk and paint, and flag anything that's cracked wide, wet or getting worse.

How to spot quality finishes

Finish work is what you see every day, so it's worth a careful look. In good light, check:

  • Walls and ceilings. Smooth and flat, with no visible seams, ridges or dents. Shine a phone flashlight along the wall at a low angle to spot problems.
  • Corners. Straight, clean inside and outside corners.
  • Trim. Tight joints at corners, nail holes filled, no gaps between trim and wall.
  • Caulk. Continuous, neat lines at counters, tubs, trim and windows.
  • Paint. Even coverage, clean lines where colors meet, no drips.
  • Doors. Even gaps around the edges, and they latch without forcing.

Most of these are normal punch list items, and builders expect to fix them.

How many nail pops are normal in drywall?

There's no universal number. A few in a new house is common. What matters is why they happen and how your warranty handles them.

A guide on drywall from Canada Mortgage and Housing Corporation explains it well. Framing lumber dries after the house is closed in and shrinks as it does. As a stud shrinks, it pulls away from the drywall, and the nail or screw head pushes out. The guide notes that problems from this kind of shrinkage are generally worse in the first year, while the house dries out.

Is it important to fix nail pops in drywall?

Not urgently. They're cosmetic. But fixing them too early can mean fixing them twice, since more can appear while the house dries.

That's why many builders handle drywall touch-ups once, near the end of the first year. Check your warranty for how and when drywall items are covered, and keep a list as you go.

Are nail pops in the ceiling normal?

Yes, in a new home they're common. The same guide describes truss uplift: the bottom of a roof truss stays warmer and drier than the top in winter, so it can bow upward slightly. That mostly shows up as small cracks where ceilings meet interior walls. They often open in winter and close in summer.

Do nail pops mean foundation problems?

Usually not. Scattered nail pops are a drying and shrinkage issue in the framing.

Signs worth a closer look:

  • Cracks wider than a hairline, or ones that keep growing
  • Diagonal cracks running from the corners of doors and windows
  • Doors and windows that suddenly stick or won't latch
  • Floors that slope noticeably
  • Any crack with moisture or staining

If you see those, report them to the builder's warranty team in writing.

Keep a running list

Walk the house once a month in the first year. Note anything new with a photo and the date. When your first-year warranty walk comes around, you'll have everything in one place.
