How a New Construction Home Gets Built: Every Stage

By Charles CarperUpdated October 20263 min read

A new home goes up in seven stages: site work, foundation, framing, rough-ins, outside finishes, inside finishes, and final steps. Local inspectors check the work along the way. Homes built for sale usually take several months from permit to completion.

What are the 7 stages of construction?

I spent almost two years on job sites, from grading to final finishes. This is the order a house goes up in. The National Association of Home Builders tracks building costs in these same stages, if you want the current breakdown.

Stage What happens
1. Site work Clearing, grading, utilities, erosion control
2. Foundation Footings, slab or crawl space, waterproofing
3. Framing Walls, floors, roof trusses and sheathing
4. Rough-ins Plumbing, electrical, and heating and cooling run through the walls
5. Outside finishes Roofing, siding, windows, doors
6. Inside finishes Insulation, drywall, trim, cabinets, flooring, paint
7. Final steps Fixtures, landscaping, driveway, cleaning, final inspection

NAHB groups insulation and drywall with inside finishes. I think of them as their own moment. Once the drywall goes up, you can't see inside the walls again.

The inspections along the way

North Carolina's building code lists the routine inspections the local building department does:

  1. Footing
  2. Under slab, where it applies
  3. Foundation
  4. Rough-in
  5. Building framing
  6. Insulation
  7. Final

A recent state ruling also says local departments can't add other routine inspections without approval.

Those inspections check that the work meets code. Many buyers also hire their own inspector, often before drywall and before closing. At Collins Ridge, you can do both with a licensed home inspector, on the build schedule.

How many man hours does it take to build a 1500 sq ft house?

There's no good standard number, and I'd be careful with anyone who gives you one. A house is built by many separate crews. Each one works a few days at a time, with gaps for inspections, deliveries and weather.

Time on the calendar is more useful. The Census Bureau publishes how long homes take from permit to completion every year, by region. Homes built for sale usually take several months.

How long are new homes built to last?

The structure is built to last for generations with normal care. The parts inside it wear out on their own schedules.

InterNACHI's chart puts an architectural shingle roof at about 30 years, a heat pump at 10 to 15 years, and a tank water heater at 6 to 12 years. Those numbers assume regular upkeep. Keep up with filters, gutters, caulk and drainage, and a new home gives you decades before the big replacements start.

What I'd watch for as a buyer

  • Before drywall: your one look inside the walls. You can see outlet spots, TV mount blocking, and plumbing and duct runs.
  • During outside finishes: the flashing around windows and doors keeps water out. It's worth a look.
  • At the end: a careful final walkthrough and punch list.
