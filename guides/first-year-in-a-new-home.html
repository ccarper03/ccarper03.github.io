Your First Year in a New Construction Home

By Charles CarperUpdated October 20262 min read

Expect small cracks and nail pops in the first year. They happen as the framing lumber dries and the house settles in, and they're cosmetic. Keep a running list. Report anything with water or structure right away.

How long does it take for a new construction home to settle?

Most of the movement happens in the first year, as the house goes through all four seasons.

What people call settling is mostly the framing lumber drying out. A drywall guide from Canada Mortgage and Housing Corporation explains that lumber shrinks as it dries, and that the problems it causes are usually worst in the first year. Some cracks open in winter, when the air is dry, and close again in summer.

What's normal in year one

  • Nail and screw pops in drywall, as the studs shrink
  • Hairline cracks at drywall corners, often where ceilings meet walls
  • Small gaps in trim and caulk
  • Small squeaks in floors or stairs
  • Doors that stick a little as the house adjusts to the humidity

These are cosmetic and common. That's why the warranty visit comes near the end of the first year, after the movement settles.

Do nail pops mean foundation problems?

Almost never on their own. Scattered nail pops come from wood drying, not the foundation.

Report these right away instead of waiting:

  • A leak, stain or water where it shouldn't be
  • Cracks that keep getting wider, or run at an angle from door and window corners
  • Doors or windows that suddenly won't close
  • Floors that clearly slope
  • Electrical, plumbing, or heating and cooling problems

The FTC notes that builder warranties usually cover major structural problems that make a home unsafe, but often leave out small cracks in drywall, tile or concrete.

Your warranty visit

Your warranty book sets the timing: when to send your first list, and when to schedule the visit near the end of your first year. Anything urgent or about safety, send in right away.

Before your visit:

  1. Walk the whole house with your running list.
  2. Add photos and dates.
  3. Send the list in writing.
  4. Keep copies of everything.

Put the date on your calendar the week you move in. It's easy to miss.

Year-one habits that help

  • Check your heating and cooling filter. ENERGY STAR suggests about once a month.
  • Keep gutters clear, and point downspouts away from the house.
  • Don't let mulch or soil pile up against the foundation.
  • Run the bath fan during and after showers.
