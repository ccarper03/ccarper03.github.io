Which Direction Should Your House Face?

By Charles CarperUpdated October 20263 min read

There's no one best direction. It depends on how you'll use the back yard and which rooms you want sun in. Where it's warm, big windows facing west get hot on summer afternoons. Windows facing south are the easiest to shade with a roof overhang. Decide where you want morning and evening light, then pick the homesite that gives it to you.

Which direction should a house face?

The way the front door faces matters less than where the sun hits the rooms and yard you use most.

Think about the back yard and the rooms you live in:

  • Back yard faces north: a shadier yard and patio. Cooler afternoons, and the front of the house gets the sun.
  • Back yard faces south: a sunny yard most of the day. Good for gardens. A patio may need shade in summer.
  • Back yard faces east: morning sun, and afternoon shade on the patio. Many people like this for summer evenings outside.
  • Back yard faces west: a shady patio in the morning and strong sun late in the day. Great sunsets, hot summer evenings.

Which side of the house facing is not good?

No side is bad. Each one has a trade-off. The one people regret most where it's hot is a lot of bare glass facing west. Late summer sun comes in low, and a roof overhang can't block it well, so those rooms can get warm.

What the Department of Energy says about sun

The Department of Energy explains how south-facing windows work. They get the most sun in winter, and a roof overhang that's the right size can shade them in summer. It says windows meant to catch sun should face within 30 degrees of true south. It also warns against too much south glass, since new homes don't need much heat. And it notes that most places need some summer shade.

You don't need a special solar home to use the idea. Shaded south windows and fewer big west windows make a home easier to keep comfortable.

Which direction is best for a house to face in Florida?

The ideas are the same anywhere hot. Limit bare glass on the west and east. Shade the south side. Put your patio where the afternoon sun won't bake it. Florida's long hot season makes these choices matter even more. In North Carolina, we have real winters too, so some winter sun through south windows is nice.

How to check a homesite

  1. Stand on the homesite and use your phone's compass to see which way the back faces.
  2. Picture where the patio and main windows will be.
  3. Come back late in the afternoon to see the sun and shade.
  4. Ask what will be built to the south and west of you. New homes and trees change the light.

At Collins Ridge, homes are built first, so on a home that's ready now, you can stand in the real rooms at the time of day you care about.
