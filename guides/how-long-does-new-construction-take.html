How Long Does New Construction Take?

By Charles CarperUpdated October 20263 min read

Homes built for sale usually take several months from permit to completion. Custom homes usually take longer. A home that's already started or finished can close much sooner. At Collins Ridge, homes are built first, so some are ready now.

Where to find the current numbers

The Census Bureau's Survey of Construction tracks how long new homes take, every year. It splits the numbers by type: homes built for sale, custom homes and homes built to rent. NAHB's Eye on Housing blog sums it up in plain English.

The pattern holds year to year. Homes built for sale finish faster than custom homes, because the builder controls the plans, the crews and the schedule.

How long does it normally take to build a 2000 sq ft house?

For a home built for sale, plan on several months from permit to completion. Size matters less than you'd think. What matters more is the stage the home is in when you buy it, the builder's schedule, and the weather.

Ready now, being built or starting soon

When the builder starts homes first, you don't always have to wait for the full build. At Collins Ridge, you pick from three kinds of homes:

  • Ready now: The home is done. You can close once your loan is ready.
  • Being built: The home is started. Your move-in date depends on how far along it is.
  • Starting soon: The plan is set for that homesite, but work hasn't started. It takes several months. Ask me for the current estimate.

A home that's halfway done might fit your timeline. Ask me what's at each stage right now.

Which stages take longest

In my experience on job sites, the waits are usually between stages, not inside them:

  • Before the start: permits and site work.
  • Inspections: each one has to pass before the next stage begins.
  • Interior finishes: drywall, cabinets, flooring, paint and trim involve the most separate trades.
  • Weather: rain stops grading, foundations, roofing and exterior work.

Why is construction so slow right now?

It may be faster than you think. Build times rise and fall with the market, and the Census Bureau publishes the current numbers.

When an individual home runs slow, the usual causes are local: weather, an inspection that needs a re-check, a back-ordered item, or a trade crew stretched across several jobs. Ask the builder what's driving a delay and what the new date is.

Is new construction slowing down?

It depends on the market and the month. The Census Bureau publishes new residential construction numbers every month, and they move up and down.

For a buyer, what matters more is what's happening in the community you're looking at: how many homes are available, how many are under construction, and what's planned next.

Planning around a build

  • Your lease: Leave a cushion. Build dates move.
  • Your rate lock: A standard lock may not cover a long build. Ask your lender about options.
  • Your current home: A build window can give you time to sell.
