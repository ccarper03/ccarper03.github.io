How Buying a New Construction Home Works, Step by Step

By Charles CarperUpdated October 20264 min read

Buying a new construction home usually takes seven steps. You talk to a lender, see the homes, pick one, pick your colors if it's early, sign, get updates, and close. I work for the builder, which is the seller. You can always bring your own agent.

How does buying a new construction home work?

Here's the path I walk people through.

  1. Talk to a lender first. Know what monthly payment feels right before you fall for a home. You can use any lender you want.
  2. Bring your agent, if you want one. Bring them to your first visit, or have them register you before you come.
  3. Come see the homes. Walk the model. Then walk real homes that are ready now or being built. At Collins Ridge, homes are built first, so you see the real thing, not just a drawing.
  4. Pick your home. Each home already has its floor plan and outside look set for its homesite. You're picking a home, not designing one.
  5. Pick your colors, if it's early. There's no design center. If the home is early in the build, you may get to pick from a few designer color schemes. Designers put each one together, so everything goes together.
  6. Sign and get updates. You sign the builder's contract and put down earnest money. I keep you posted while your home gets finished. You can also hire your own licensed inspector before drywall and again before closing.
  7. Walk through it and close. We do a final walkthrough together. Then you close with an attorney and get your keys.

Why are there fewer choices?

Big production builders use the same floor plans over and over, so the plans are tested. Here, the finishes are already picked by designers.

That means fewer decisions and fewer delays. You won't spend weeks choosing tile and cabinets.

How long does it take?

It depends on which home you pick.

  • Ready now. The home is done. You can close once your loan is ready.
  • Being built. The home is started. Your move-in date depends on how far along it is.
  • Starting soon. The plan is set for that homesite, but work hasn't started. It takes several months to build, so ask me for the current estimate. That gives you time to sell your home or finish your lease.

Can you look at new homes without a realtor?

Yes. The model is open to the public, and you can walk in on your own.

Just know two things. First, I work for the builder, which is the seller. Second, if you want an agent to represent you, bring them to your first visit or have them register you before you come.

Should I get a realtor if I'm buying a new build?

It's your call, and it's a fair question. I work for the builder, so here's the honest version.

A good agent can read the contract with you, help you compare homes, and be a second set of eyes on something big.

If you don't use an agent, you can still protect yourself. Hire a closing attorney, get your own licensed inspection, and read every page before you sign.

What I'd avoid is deciding halfway. If you want an agent, bring them in at the start.

Who pays the realtor when buying a new build house?

That's between you and your agent, and it's spelled out in your written buyer agreement. Ask your agent to walk you through it.

The builder I sell for works with buyers' agents. If you have one, bring them in from your first visit.

What happens if I don't have an agent?

You buy on your own. I still help you through every step, but I work for the builder.

That's a normal way to buy a new home. Just cover the gaps yourself:

  • Read the contract carefully, and think about having an attorney review it.
  • Ask what comes with the home, and get the features list in writing.
  • Ask about inspections, timing and the warranty up front.
  • Compare Loan Estimates from more than one lender.

Who's writing this. I sell new D.R. Horton homes, so I work for the builder, which is the seller. This guide is general education from my time on job sites and in sales. Every builder does some things differently, so ask yours how it works there. It isn't legal, tax or lending advice.
