What Is a Pre-Drywall Walkthrough?

By Charles CarperUpdated October 20263 min read

A pre-drywall walkthrough happens after the framing, plumbing, electrical, and heating and cooling are in, but before insulation and drywall cover them. It's your one chance to see inside the walls. It's worth doing, ideally with your own licensed inspector.

Is a pre-drywall inspection worth it?

Yes. I've stood in a lot of framed houses, and this is the step I'd least want a buyer to skip.

Once insulation and drywall go up, the framing, wiring, pipes and ducts are hidden for good. InterNACHI says it plainly: fixing problems after a home is finished is hard and expensive. Before drywall, many of the same fixes are quick.

There are two kinds:

  • A builder walkthrough. Some builders walk buyers through the home at this stage to look at the layout and what's been put in. Ask me if one is planned for your home.
  • A private inspection. Your own licensed home inspector looks at the work for you. At Collins Ridge, you can do this. We can't stop work for it, so tell me early and I'll help you line it up with our superintendent.

What happens during a pre-drywall inspection?

InterNACHI says the inspection happens after the foundation, framing, windows, doors, plumbing and electrical are in, but before drywall. Some inspectors also look after insulation goes in, which shows how well that was done.

Inspectors often check:

  • Framing: wall studs, floor joists, holes and notches cut in the wood, and extra support where it's needed
  • Structure: the footings, and any missing metal clips or connectors
  • Water: flashing around windows and doors, and waterproofing
  • Plumbing: where the pipes run, and a drain pan under the washer spot
  • Electrical: wiring, box locations, and enough outlets in each room
  • Heating and cooling: where the ducts and vents are
  • Fire safety: fire-blocking material where it belongs

InterNACHI also suggests photos and video. I'd take your own, too.

What to check yourself

Even if you hire an inspector, walk the house with your own list:

  • Outlets and switches where you'll really use them. Think about your bed, desk, TV and kitchen counters.
  • TV and cable spots, and blocking in the wall if you'll mount a TV.
  • Blocking for grab bars, shelves or heavy mirrors.
  • Ceiling fan spots with the right support.
  • The plan. Make sure what's framed matches the floor plan.

Take photos of every wall in every room. Years from now, when you want to hang something heavy or run a wire, you'll know where the studs and pipes are.

How long does a pre-drywall inspection take?

Usually a few hours, depending on the size of the home and the inspector. Plan enough time to walk every room without rushing.

Timing matters

The window before drywall is short. Drywall often follows insulation within days. So tell me early that you want an inspection, and I'll connect you with our superintendent for the timing.

You or your inspector can send the report straight to our superintendent. If something doesn't meet code, we'll take care of it. For other notes, our superintendent decides what we can do and gets back to you.
