Why Buyers Choose New Construction

By Charles CarperUpdated October 20263 min read

People choose new construction for the condition, the layout, the efficiency, the warranty, and the chance to walk the real home before they buy. The trade-offs are time, lot size and a neighborhood still filling in. It's a good choice when those benefits matter more to you than the trade-offs.

What are the benefits of buying new construction?

The ones buyers mention to me most:

  • Nothing's worn out. Appraisers give a never-lived-in home the top condition rating. Roof, HVAC, water heater and appliances all start their clocks at zero.
  • A layout built for now. Open kitchens, more bathrooms, flex rooms and storage that older plans often lack.
  • Efficiency. New homes are built to current energy codes.
  • A warranty. Builders provide a written warranty on certain items for set periods.
  • Simple choices. At Collins Ridge, you pick a home that's built or underway. If it's early, you may pick from a few designer color schemes. Fewer decisions, faster move.
  • Timing you can plan around. A home being built can line up with a lease ending or a sale.

What are some good reasons to buy a new home?

The best reasons are about your life, not the market:

  • Your household is growing or shrinking, and your home doesn't fit anymore.
  • You're tired of renting and ready to stay put for a while.
  • You want to stop putting money into repairs on an aging house.
  • A job or a move sets your timeline.
  • You want one-level living, a main-floor bedroom, or space for family.

NAR's yearly profile of buyers and sellers shows that people tend to stay in their homes for many years. Most people buy to stay. Buy a home that fits the next chapter, not just this year.

What to consider when buying a new construction home?

The trade-offs, honestly:

  • Time. A home that's starting soon takes several months. Here, homes are built first, so some are ready sooner.
  • Lot size. Many new homes sit on smaller lots than older homes.
  • The neighborhood is still forming. Construction, young trees and amenities in progress.
  • The HOA. Most new communities have one, often run by the developer at first.
  • The model isn't standard. Ask what's included.

What does Warren Buffett say about buying a home?

In his 2010 letter to Berkshire Hathaway shareholders, Buffett said home ownership makes sense for most Americans, but he didn't treat a home mainly as an investment. He said he'd likely have made more money renting and investing, yet he valued the decades his family spent in the house he bought.

He also warned that a house turns into a nightmare when a buyer's eyes are bigger than his wallet. His point was that families should buy homes they can afford, not their dream houses. I agree with that, and I sell houses for a living.
