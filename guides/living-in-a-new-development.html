Pros and Cons of Living in a New Development

By Charles CarperUpdated October 20263 min read

The upside is a new home, new streets and amenities, efficient building and neighbors moving in at the same time. The downsides are construction nearby for a while, small trees, an HOA with rules, and a neighborhood that's still taking shape.

What are the disadvantages of a PUD?

A PUD, or planned unit development, is a community planned as a whole. It usually has common areas owned and run by a homeowners association. The common downsides:

  • HOA dues and rules. You pay for common areas, and you follow rules on things like fences, paint colors and parking.
  • The developer runs the HOA at first. North Carolina law lets the declaration set a period of developer control. Owners must then elect a board, with a majority of owners on it, no later than when it ends.
  • Homes look alike. Design rules keep the community looking consistent. Some people love that, and some don't.
  • Smaller lots. Many planned communities use smaller lots to make room for shared space.

What are the advantages and disadvantages of new construction homes?

Advantages

  • Nothing's worn out. The systems and appliances start new.
  • Layouts built for how people live now.
  • Built to today's energy codes.
  • A builder warranty on certain items.
  • When homes are built first, as they are at Collins Ridge, you can see the real home before you buy.

Disadvantages

  • Construction nearby until the community is built out.
  • Young trees and new landscaping.
  • Amenities may not be done yet.
  • A wait if the home hasn't started yet.
  • Less room on the base price.

The things people don't expect

What I hear from buyers once they've moved in:

  • Construction noise and traffic during work hours, for months or longer, depending on the phase.
  • Dust and mud near homesites being built.
  • Views that change. An empty lot behind you becomes a home.
  • New neighbors at the same time. Many people move in around the same time, which can make it easier to meet people.

Is building a house worth it right now?

It's worth it when the benefits matter to you and the timing works. If you want a brand-new home with nothing to fix for a while, and you can live with a wait or a neighborhood still being built, a new home is a strong choice. If you need a big yard, big trees or a move next month, a resale home may fit better. A home that's ready now can close sooner, too.

How to judge a development before you buy

  • Ask for the community plan, and what's planned around your homesite.
  • Read the HOA declaration, rules and budget.
  • Ask when the amenities will be done.
  • Visit during work hours to see and hear the construction.
  • Walk a finished section to see what the neighborhood will become.
