Should I Buy a House Now or Wait?

By Charles CarperUpdated October 20263 min read

Nobody can time the market for you, including me. The better question is whether your life is ready: a stable job, savings left after closing, a payment you're comfortable with, and plans to stay long enough for buying costs to pay off.

Is it better to buy a house now or wait?

It depends on you more than the market. I won't predict rates or prices. Nobody can do that reliably, and anyone selling homes has a reason to tell you now is the time. That includes me.

What I can do is give you the questions that decide it.

How to tell if it's a good time to buy a house

Ask yourself these five, in order:

  1. How long will I stay? Buying and selling both cost money. The longer you stay, the more those costs get spread out. If you might move again in a year or two, renting may make more sense.
  2. Is my income steady? A lender will look at your job history. So should you.
  3. What's left after closing? Your down payment and closing costs shouldn't empty your savings. Keep a cushion.
  4. What payment am I comfortable with? Not the most a lender will approve. The number you'd be fine with in a tight month.
  5. What's pushing me? A lease ending, a growing family, a new job or orders. Life events set real deadlines. Market headlines don't.

If you can answer all five with confidence, timing matters less than you think.

Is 2026 a bad time to buy a house?

Every year has its headlines. Rates, prices and inventory all move, and they move differently in every town.

Here's a more useful way to think about it. If buying fits your life and your budget today, waiting carries its own costs: another year of rent, a deadline you have to meet anyway, or a price that may go either way. If buying doesn't fit yet, waiting is the right call, and there's no shame in it.

What waiting gets you

Waiting can be smart when it buys you something concrete:

  • More savings, so you keep a cushion after closing
  • A better credit score, which can affect your rate
  • Clarity on a job or relocation
  • Time to sell your current home first

Waiting just to see what the market does rarely gets you anything you can count on.

What buying now gets you

  • Your own space, on your terms
  • A fixed housing cost, if you choose a fixed-rate loan
  • Equity that builds as you pay down the loan
  • On new construction, the chance to pick your plan and finishes

A middle path for new construction

New construction has one timing advantage. A home being built can take months to finish. The Census Bureau's data shows homes built for sale took about 7.4 months from permit to completion in 2025.

That can line up with a lease ending, a planned move or the sale of your current home. It also means you need to plan your rate lock and savings around the build schedule. Talk to a lender early, and ask how they handle longer timelines.

Where I'd start

Get a lender to run your real numbers. Then write down your answers to the five questions above. If they point to yes, start looking. If they point to not yet, set a date to check again.
