Should I Buy a House Now or Wait?

By Charles CarperUpdated October 20263 min read

No one can time the market for you, me included. The better question is whether your life is ready. A steady job, savings left after closing, a payment you're comfortable with, and plans to stay long enough for the cost of buying to pay off.

Is it better to buy a house now or wait?

It depends on you more than the market. I won't guess where rates or prices are going. No one can do that well, and anyone who sells homes has a reason to tell you now is the time. That includes me.

What I can do is give you the questions that decide it.

How to tell if it's a good time to buy a house

Ask yourself these five, in order:

  1. How long will I stay? Buying and selling both cost money. The longer you stay, the more those costs get spread out. If you might move again in a year or two, renting may make more sense.
  2. Is my income steady? A lender will look at your work history. So should you.
  3. What's left after closing? Your down payment and closing costs shouldn't empty your savings. Keep some money set aside.
  4. What payment am I comfortable with? Not the most a lender will approve. The number you'd be fine with in a tight month.
  5. What's pushing me? A lease ending, a growing family, a new job or military orders. Life sets real deadlines. News headlines don't.

If you feel good about all five answers, timing matters less than you think.

Is now a bad time to buy a house?

Every year has its headlines. Rates, prices and the number of homes for sale all move, and they move differently in every town.

Here's a better way to think about it. If buying fits your life and your budget today, waiting has costs too. Another year of rent. A deadline you have to meet anyway. A price that could go up or down. If buying doesn't fit yet, waiting is the right call, and that's OK.

What waiting gets you

Waiting can be smart when it gets you something real:

  • More savings, so you still have money set aside after closing
  • A better credit score, which can affect your rate
  • A clear answer on a job or a move
  • Time to sell your current home first

Waiting just to see what the market does rarely gets you anything you can count on.

What buying now gets you

  • Your own place, your way
  • A housing cost that stays the same, if you pick a fixed-rate loan
  • Equity that grows as you pay down the loan
  • With a new home, a home where everything starts new

A middle path with new construction

At Collins Ridge, homes are started before they're sold. So you can pick a home based on when you need to move:

  • Ready now if you need to move soon.
  • Being built if you have a few months.
  • Starting soon if your date is further out.

A home that hasn't started yet takes several months to build. That can line up with a lease ending, a planned move, or selling your current home. Just plan your rate lock and savings around the build. Talk to a lender early, and ask how they handle a longer timeline.

Where I'd start

Have a lender run your real numbers. Then write down your answers to the five questions above. If they point to yes, start looking. If they point to not yet, pick a date to check again.
