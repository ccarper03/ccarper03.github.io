Mortgage Rate Locks on New Construction, Explained

By Charles CarperUpdated October 20263 min read

A rate lock holds your interest rate from your loan offer until closing, as long as you close in time and nothing changes on your application. On a home still being built, the build schedule decides how long a lock you need. On a home that's ready now, a regular lock often covers you.

What a rate lock does

The Consumer Financial Protection Bureau (CFPB) puts it simply. A rate lock means your interest rate won't change between the offer and closing, as long as you close before the lock runs out and nothing changes on your application.

Without a lock, your rate can go up or down any day until you close.

How soon can I lock in a new mortgage rate?

Usually once you have a home under contract and a loan application in. Locks last a set number of days. Your Loan Estimate shows when yours ends.

This is where the home you pick matters. On a home that's ready now, a regular lock often covers you. On a home still being built, it might not. A home that hasn't started yet takes several months to build. You don't want a short lock on a home that closes months from now.

Lenders handle this a few ways:

  • Lock later. Wait until closing is close enough for a regular lock.
  • A longer lock. Lock early for a longer time, often for a fee.
  • Builder or lender programs. Some offer longer locks for homes being built.

Ask your lender which ones they offer and what each costs. Many big builders have an affiliated lender. You can use them or anyone else, so compare.

At what point is a mortgage rate locked in?

When the lender confirms the lock. Get it in writing. Your Loan Estimate shows if your rate is locked and when the lock ends.

Even after you lock, the CFPB says your rate can still change if:

  • You change your loan type or down payment
  • The appraisal comes in higher or lower than expected
  • Your credit score changes
  • The lender can't confirm overtime, bonus or other income

While your home is being built, keep your money life steady. No new car loans, no new credit cards, and no furniture on credit until after closing.

How much does it cost to lock your rate?

It depends. Many lenders build regular lock periods into their pricing. Longer locks and extensions can cost more. The CFPB warns that adding time to a lock can be expensive.

Your Loan Estimate shows if you're locked and for how long. It doesn't show what more time would cost, or what other lock lengths cost. So ask.

Can you get a lower interest rate on a new build?

Not just because it's new. Your rate depends on the market, your credit, your loan type and your down payment.

What a new home sometimes comes with is a builder incentive aimed at your rate, like a buydown. That's money used to lower your rate or payment, not a different market rate. Compare it with other lenders' offers before you decide. At Collins Ridge, ask me what's available on the home you like.

Questions to ask before you lock

The CFPB suggests the first six. I'd add the last two for a new home:

  1. What does it mean if I lock my rate today?
  2. How long is the lock on this Loan Estimate?
  3. Can I get a shorter or longer lock, and what does it cost?
  4. What happens if my closing is late and the lock runs out?
  5. What could still change my rate?
  6. If I lock and rates drop, what happens?
  7. When does the builder expect this home to close, and how firm is that date?
  8. Do you have a lock made for homes still being built?
