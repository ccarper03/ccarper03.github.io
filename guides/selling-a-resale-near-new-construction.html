Selling a Resale Home Near New Construction

By Charles CarperUpdated October 20263 min read

Compete on what new construction can't offer: a faster move-in, a finished yard, mature trees, upgrades already included and a price without lot premiums or design-center extras. Price against the new homes' total cost, not their base price, and show buyers the comparison.

Where a resale home wins

I sell for a builder, so here's an honest list of where your listing has the edge:

  • Move-in speed. A home started from scratch averaged about 7.4 months from permit to completion in 2025, per Census Bureau data. Your listing can close in weeks.
  • A finished yard. Mature trees, landscaping, a fence, maybe a deck.
  • Included extras. Blinds, appliances, a finished patio, garage shelving. On new construction, those often cost extra.
  • A larger lot. Many new homes sit on smaller lots. In 2025, 38% of new homes sold were on lots under 7,000 square feet.
  • No construction nearby.

Price against total cost, not base price

Buyers compare your list price to a builder's base price. That's not a fair fight, so reframe it. A new home's real cost often includes:

  • A lot premium on some homesites
  • Design center options
  • Blinds, appliances and fencing
  • Landscaping beyond the basics

Build a side-by-side for buyers: your home's price with what's included, against the new home's base price plus the items they'd add. Then note the builder's incentives honestly, because buyers will hear about them.

Do new builds have good resale value?

Generally, yes, when they're maintained and priced in line with the neighborhood. For your listing, the bigger question is whether your home's condition holds up. Appraisers rate condition from C1 for new and never occupied down to C6. A well-kept resale with updated systems competes well.

Are new construction homes hard to sell?

When a home is a few years old and the builder is still selling new homes nearby, the builder's incentives are the main competition. That's manageable if you lead with what the new home doesn't have, and if the condition is strong.

What is the hardest month to sell a house?

Seasonality varies by market. Activity typically slows in late fall and winter and picks up in spring, but well-priced homes sell year-round. Check your local MLS data for when homes in your area sell fastest, and price for the season you're in.

Positioning tips

  1. Lead the listing with move-in timing and what's included.
  2. Show the yard, trees and outdoor space.
  3. Have the age of the roof, HVAC and water heater ready.
  4. Offer a pre-listing inspection, if it fits your strategy.
  5. Know the nearby builder's current incentives so you're never surprised.
