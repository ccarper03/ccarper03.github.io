How to Decide on a House Without Second-Guessing

By Charles CarperUpdated October 20263 min read

Set your budget and your three must-haves before you look. Judge every home against them, and walk away from real red flags. Second-guessing usually comes from deciding what you want after you've fallen for a house.

What is the first thing you should do to buy a house?

Figure out your budget. Not the most a lender will approve, but the payment you're comfortable with.

The Consumer Financial Protection Bureau's homebuying guide starts the same way. Look at your budget and what matters to you first, then look at loans. Talking to a lender turns your budget into a real price range. Everything else gets easier from there.

Sort your must-haves

Second-guessing usually starts when people decide what they want after they fall for a house. Do it the other way around.

Before you visit anything, make three lists:

  1. Must-haves (three at most). Things you won't give up. A bedroom on the main floor. A drive to work under 30 minutes. A payment under a set number.
  2. Strong wants. Things you'd trade for a must-have. A bigger yard. A flex room. A covered patio.
  3. Nice-to-haves. Things you'd enjoy but won't pay much for.

Then judge every home against the lists, in that order. If a home misses a must-have, it's out, no matter how good the kitchen looks.

What are the top 5 things to consider when buying a house?

  1. The full monthly cost. Payment, taxes, insurance, HOA dues, utilities and upkeep.
  2. Where it is, and the drive. Drive it at rush hour before you decide.
  3. The layout. Does it fit how you live on a normal Tuesday, not just on a holiday?
  4. Condition. On a resale home, how old the roof, heat pump and water heater are. On a new home, what's included and how the build gets inspected.
  5. How long you'll stay. Buying and selling cost money. The longer you stay, the more it makes sense.

At Collins Ridge, homes are built first. So you can walk the real home, not just a model, and check it against your lists.

What are the biggest red flags to avoid when buying a house?

Walk away, or slow way down, if you see:

  • A payment that only works if nothing goes wrong. With no money set aside, there's no room for surprises.
  • Pressure to decide today. A good choice will still be good after a night's sleep.
  • Water problems. Stains, soft spots, standing water, or poor drainage around the home.
  • Big promises that aren't in writing. If it matters, it belongs in the contract.
  • A missing must-have. You'll notice it every day.

How to finish the decision

When you're down to two homes, do three things:

  1. Put both through your three lists, side by side.
  2. Have your lender work out the full monthly cost of each one.
  3. Visit each one again at a different time of day.

Then pick one, and stop shopping. Looking at listings after you've decided is where second-guessing lives.
