How to Decide on a House Without Second-Guessing

By Charles CarperUpdated October 20263 min read

Decide your budget and your three non-negotiables before you look, judge every home against them, and walk away from real red flags. Second-guessing usually comes from deciding the criteria after falling for a house.

What is the first thing you should do to buy a house?

Figure out your budget. Not the most a lender will approve, but the payment you're comfortable with.

The CFPB's homebuying guide starts the same way: assess your budget and priorities first, then explore loan options. A lender conversation turns your budget into a real price range. Everything else gets easier from there.

The must-have sort

Second-guessing usually starts when people set their criteria after they fall for a house. Do it the other way around.

Before you visit anything, write three lists:

  1. Non-negotiables (three, at most). Things you won't compromise on. A bedroom on the main floor. A commute under 30 minutes. A payment under a set number.
  2. Strong preferences. Things you'd trade for a non-negotiable. A bigger yard. A flex room. A covered patio.
  3. Nice-to-haves. Things you'd enjoy but won't pay much for.

Then judge every home against the lists, in that order. If a home misses a non-negotiable, it's out, no matter how good the kitchen looks.

What are the top 5 things to consider when buying a house?

  1. The total monthly cost. Payment, taxes, insurance, HOA dues, utilities and upkeep.
  2. Location and commute. Drive it at rush hour before you decide.
  3. The layout. Does it fit how you live on an ordinary Tuesday, not just a holiday?
  4. Condition. For a resale home, the age of the roof, HVAC and water heater. For new construction, what's included and how the build is inspected.
  5. How long you'll stay. Buying and selling cost money. The longer you stay, the more it makes sense.

What are the biggest red flags to avoid when buying a house?

Walk away, or slow way down, if you see:

  • A payment that only works if everything goes right. No cushion means no margin.
  • Pressure to decide today. A good decision survives a night's sleep.
  • Water problems. Stains, soft spots, standing water or poor drainage around the home.
  • Big promises that aren't in writing. If it matters, it belongs in the contract.
  • Missing a non-negotiable. You'll notice it every day.

How to finish the decision

When you're down to two homes, do three things:

  1. Put both through your three lists, side by side.
  2. Price the full monthly cost of each with your lender.
  3. Visit each one again at a different time of day.

Then pick, and stop shopping. Looking at listings after you've decided is where second-guessing lives.
