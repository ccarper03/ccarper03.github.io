Living in Hillsborough, NC: Honest Pros and Cons

By Charles CarperUpdated October 20264 min read

Hillsborough is a small, historic town in Orange County, about 20 minutes from Durham and Chapel Hill. It has a walkable downtown, with parks and the Eno River close by. The trade-offs are a longer drive to Raleigh, fewer big stores in town, and home prices that match the location.

Is Hillsborough, NC a good place to live?

I work here every day, so this is my honest take. It's a good fit if you want a small town with a real downtown and a quick drive to Durham and Chapel Hill. It's a harder fit if your daily drive points toward Raleigh or RTP.

It's a small town with a short average commute. For the current population and commute numbers, see the Census Bureau's QuickFacts page for Hillsborough.

The pros

  • A historic, walkable downtown. Hillsborough is one of the older towns in North Carolina. Downtown has shops, places to eat and historic sites, all a short walk from public parking.
  • Outdoors close by. From the Collins Ridge sales office, where I work, Gold Park is about 6 minutes, Occoneechee Mountain State Natural Area about 5, and an Eno River access point about 13.
  • Durham and Chapel Hill are close. Off-peak drive times I measured from the sales office: Duke University Hospital about 18 minutes, the Duke Chapel about 21, Franklin Street in Chapel Hill about 23, and UNC Hospitals in Chapel Hill about 26.
  • A hospital in town. UNC Hospitals Hillsborough is about 9 minutes from the sales office.
  • Buses to nearby cities. GoTriangle runs regional buses to Durham and Chapel Hill, and the town has a local bus. Routes change, so check GoTriangle's trip planner.

Things to do

  • The Riverwalk. An accessible path along the Eno River that connects to Occoneechee Mountain State Natural Area.
  • Eno River State Park. Miles of hiking trails, plus fishing.
  • History. The Orange County Historical Museum, the Burwell School Historic Site and the Occaneechi Village replica are all in town.
  • Downtown evenings. The town and its arts council host regular events downtown. Check the town calendar for what's coming up.

The cons

  • Raleigh is a real drive. Downtown Raleigh is about 37 miles and 53 minutes off-peak from the sales office. RTP is about 31 minutes. Add time at rush hour.
  • Small-town shopping. You have groceries and a Walmart Supercenter a few minutes away. Bigger shopping trips often mean Durham or Mebane. The Streets at Southpoint is about 26 minutes off-peak.
  • It isn't cheap. See below.

Is Hillsborough, NC expensive to live?

Housing is the biggest part, and it costs more here than in many smaller North Carolina towns. For current home values and rents, see the Census Bureau's QuickFacts page for Hillsborough.

Property taxes add a town rate on top of Orange County's. Orange County lists its rate per $100 of assessed value, plus a Hillsborough town rate for homes inside town limits.

Which city in NC has the lowest cost of living?

It depends on how you measure it, and the answer changes as prices change. The fairest way to compare is county by county, for the same kind of household. The MIT Living Wage Calculator does that for every county in North Carolina.

Lower costs usually mean a longer drive to jobs and services. Weigh the drive along with the price.

How can I travel from Hillsborough, NC to Raleigh?

Most people drive, east on I-40 or I-85 toward Durham and then on to Raleigh. From the Collins Ridge sales office, downtown Raleigh is about 53 minutes off-peak.

By bus, you'd connect through Durham on GoTriangle's regional routes. Check GoTriangle's trip planner for current routes and times.

What I'd do before deciding

Drive your real commute at the time you'd really drive it. Walk downtown on a weekday evening and a Saturday morning. Then decide if the trade-offs fit your life.
